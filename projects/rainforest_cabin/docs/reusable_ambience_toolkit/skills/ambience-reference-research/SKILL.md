# Ambience Reference Research

## Inputs

Approved concept, supplied reference image/video, camera position, climate/time,
must-have/excluded elements, asset budget and commercial-use requirements.

## Workflow

1. Inspect references at full size. List camera scale, materials, architecture,
   leaf silhouettes, wet/dry boundaries, light direction, depth and weather cues.
2. Gather real photographs/video for the difficult components. Keep source URLs
   and notes showing what each teaches. A practical board may contain 8–20 chosen
   references; more search results are not automatically better research.
3. Separate research-only inspiration from assets actually inserted into a video.
   Google Images is not a reuse license. Check individual asset/license pages.
4. Prefer existing authorized assets and high-quality CC0/PD or appropriate CC BY
   sources. Assess stock/free commercial licenses individually. Reject NC for
   commercial use without separate permission; do not assume redistribution rights.
5. Inspect actual camera-distance quality: scale, silhouettes, texture density,
   alpha edges, PBR maps, normal response, geometry and tiling. Search for a better
   ready-made asset before settling for an obviously weak procedural substitute.
6. Record author/title/source/license/version/link, evidence date, hashes, edits,
   local paths and actual version use. Login/download restrictions are real
   blockers; do not invent successful downloads or expose credentials.

## Outputs and acceptance

Deliver a reference board, detail checklist, asset register, license evidence and
an honest realism gap list. State which assets require login/payment or have
visible limits. Do not call a generated concept image a real-world photograph.
