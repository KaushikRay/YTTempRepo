# Ambience Scene Production

## Inputs

Brief, reference board, approved camera/composition, rights-cleared assets, target
renderer/hardware, visual method and native output settings.

## Workflow

1. Block out real-world dimensions, structural support, shelter, foreground,
   focal subject and depth. Remove unintended view-blocking trunks/posts.
2. Compare 1–3 compositions where useful, then lock the chosen camera and record
   changes that invalidate projections, masks, caches or downstream renders.
3. Build plausible construction, beveled edges, contact and purposeful dressing.
   Age materials according to exposure; avoid uniformly polished or noisy surfaces.
4. Calibrate PBR color spaces, texture scale, roughness, normal/displacement and
   wetness. More polygons or samples do not repair incorrect materials.
5. Give foreground plants curved, species-consistent silhouettes, believable
   attachment and transmission. Check repeated clones and rectangular leaf cards.
6. Treat glass as a real optical boundary: thickness/thin model, IOR, reflection,
   transmitted view, frame seals and appropriate condensation/water layers.
7. Establish physically plausible ambient/practical lighting and one deliberate
   color-management pipeline. Preserve warm highlights and readable dark textures;
   do not use saturation or excessive blur as a substitute for realism.
8. Render native detail crops and a whole-scene still. Put it beside references
   and list remaining limits. Distinguish actual scene renders from illustrative
   concept images and generated plates.

## Outputs and acceptance

Scene/scripts, asset manifests, camera/config record, actual still, detail crops
and reference comparison. Obtain the agreed still review before expensive motion
work, unless the user has waived that gate. Do not claim full 3D for a hybrid.
